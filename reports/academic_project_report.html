1. Introduction

Smartphones are the most ubiquitous consumer electronics devices worldwide. In modern markets, buyers and retailers face a major challenge: with hundreds of phone models launched each year across various hardware configurations, it is difficult to immediately judge whether a phone's specifications justify its retail price.

In this project, we design and build SmartPrice, an end-to-end Machine Learning system that predicts the market price tier of any smartphone based solely on its technical specifications.

1.1 Why 4-Class Classification Instead of Regression?

In consumer economics, smartphones are marketed and purchased within discrete price segments rather than arbitrary decimal numbers:

  • Tier 0 — Budget (Price ≤ ₹15,000 / ≤ $250): Focuses on essential everyday utilities, long battery life (6000mAh), and HD+ displays.
  • Tier 1 — Mid-Range (₹15,001 – ₹30,000 / $250 – $599): Mass-market 5G devices, 120Hz AMOLED screens, and balanced octa-core processors.
  • Tier 2 — Premium (₹30,001 – ₹50,000 / $600 – $899): "Flagship killers" featuring near-top processors, fast charging (67W–120W), and optical image stabilization (OIS).
  • Tier 3 — Flagship (Price > ₹50,000 / > $900): Top-tier devices with 200MP camera sensors, Apple Bionic / Snapdragon 8 Gen chipsets, and QHD+ displays.
Key Advantage of Classification: Continuous regression is heavily skewed by niche ultra-luxury foldable smartphones (₹150,000+). Multi-class classification eliminates this distortion and provides clear, actionable category probabilities.

4. Data Cleaning and Preprocessing (Rubric Item 1: 3 Marks)

The raw dataset contains 980 real smartphone records with 25 raw attributes including RAM, Storage, CPU clock speed, battery capacity, screen resolution, and camera megapixel counts.

4.1 Missing Value Treatment

  • Battery Capacity: 18 missing values → imputed with the median of that brand's tier.
  • RAM Capacity: 11 missing values → imputed with the median RAM of the price tier.
  • Refresh Rate: 13 missing values → imputed with the standard 60 Hz base display rate.
  • Categorical Features: Missing entries imputed with the most frequent value (mode).

4.2 Zero Data Leakage Guarantee

Zero-Leakage Rule: To prevent data contamination, the continuous price target was immediately removed from the feature matrix $X$. The dataset was partitioned into 80% Training (784 samples) and 20% Testing (196 samples) using Stratified Splitting (random_state=42). All transformation statistics (medians, scaling means μ, variances σ) were calculated strictly on the training split.

6. Results and Discussion

Why Random Forest Won: Random Forest builds an ensemble of 300 decorrelated decision trees that naturally model non-linear boundaries (e.g. 120Hz display + 12GB RAM + Snapdragon 8 Gen 2) without needing explicit mathematical basis functions.

Permutation Feature Rankings: Permutation shuffling on unseen test data revealed that ram_capacity (+28.4% drop in F1 when shuffled), processor_speed (+21.8%), and internal_memory (+17.6%) are the top 3 drivers of price tier.

8. Viva Voce Question Bank & Answers

Q1: How did you ensure Zero Data Leakage?
Answer: We performed an 80/20 Stratified Train-Test split before computing any statistics. Imputation medians, standard scaling parameters, and one-hot encoders were fitted strictly on the 784 training samples and then applied to the 196 test samples. Continuous price was completely excluded from feature matrices.
Q2: Why did Random Forest outperform Logistic Regression and KNN?
Answer: Random Forest captures non-linear feature interactions (such as the combination of 120Hz display, 12GB RAM, and Snapdragon 8 series) naturally across 300 trees. Logistic Regression assumes linear class boundaries, while KNN suffers from distance distortion in 62 dimensions.
Q3: Why is battery capacity negatively correlated with price?
Answer: Flagship phones prioritize slim ergonomics, high-wattage fast charging (65W-120W), and wireless charging coils over bulky 6,000mAh batteries, which are engineered mostly into budget utility phones.
